# Edge Cases and Dry Runs

**Revision 2** — the reduced-pool rule is incorporated. Part 1 is the
analysis. Part 3 is the edge matrix. The hand-traced games that used to be
Part 2 have been replaced by `dry-run.md`, which is generated by the engine
itself.

---

# Part 1 — Findings

## 1. Pools smaller than 5 cards evaluate the whole pool (resolved by rule)

The earlier revision flagged that a pool of fewer than five cards makes every
claim false, which happens in round 1 of a 4-player game and in the
two-player endgame. That is now settled by rule: the complete available pool
is evaluated instead. The consequences that remain:

- **Round 1 of a 4-player game is playable.** The 4-card pool classifies as
  something, and that classification is a legal claim, so at least one true
  claim always exists for whoever owns a card in its pattern.
- **Claim truth is claimant-*independent* below five cards.** The evaluated
  hand is the whole pool, and every in-play player holds at least one card in
  it, so the single ownership rule — own a card of the evaluated hand — is
  always satisfied. With the pool `8♠(A) 8♥(B) K♦(C) 3♣(D)`, `Pair of 8s` is
  TRUE for all four players, and every other claim is FALSE for all four. A
  reduced round has exactly one true claim and it belongs to everybody.
  Ownership only does real work at five or more cards, where the evaluated
  hand is a proper subset that can leave the claimant out.
- **The reachable category set narrows with the pool.** Four cards reach
  High Card, Pair, Two Pair, Trips and Quads. Three cards reach High Card,
  Pair and Trips. Two cards reach High Card and Pair. Claims outside that set
  are still legal to make — bluffing a Flush at four cards is allowed — they
  are simply always false.
- **Reduced mode recurs late, not just at the start.** After an elimination
  the pool shrinks: scenario 15 of the dry run ends a round with a 9-card
  pool and begins the next with a 4-card pool, because the eliminated player
  took five cards out of circulation.
- **A pool below two cards cannot occur**, since the game ends when one
  player remains, so High Card 2 is unreachable and excluded from the claim
  space.

## 2. Poker combinatorics exclude some parameter values

Not every `(category, parameter)` pair is realizable by *any* hand, quite
apart from what was dealt: Flush below 7-high, Straight below 5-high,
Straight Flush to Ace, `8s full of 8s` and `Pair of 1s` are impossible in
principle. They are claim-*validity* questions, not claim-*truth* questions,
and are rejected by `Claim.Validate()` before the evaluator ever runs.

Realizability is now judged across widths 2 to 5, not just 5, which is what
takes the claim space from 363 to **367**: High Card 3, 4, 5 and 6 become
reachable in reduced mode. Excluding them would leave reduced deals with no
true legal claim at all. Details and the reversal flag in `claim-model.md`
§4.1.

## 3. A stronger hand shadows a weaker claim when the pool is tight

Your §27 rule has a sharp consequence that only bites at small pool sizes.
With a 5-card pool there is exactly one 5-subset, so the claim must match
that hand's classification *exactly*. With 6+ cards the claimant gains
freedom to exclude cards and shed category strength. Concretely, the same
three 8s validate `Pair of 8s` at pool size 6 and refute it at pool size 5.
This is intended behaviour, not a bug, and it deserves to be explained in
the player-facing rules because it will surprise people.

## 4. Category inflation is the likely evaluator bug

The filler-distinctness constraints are what stop a `Pair` claim being
satisfied by a two-pair selection, a `Trips` claim by a full-house
selection, or a `Straight` claim by a straight-flush selection. These are
easy to omit and produce a validator that looks right in the common case.
Mitigation is the differential test against the exhaustive oracle across the
full 363-claim space (`show-validation.md` §3.3).

## 5. Card supply is comfortable

Maximum simultaneous deal is 9 players × 5 cards = **45 of 52**. The deck
never exhausts, so there is no deck-exhaustion rule to design. Maximum pool
for validation is 45 cards, giving `C(45,5) = 1,221,759` subsets for the
exhaustive oracle — fast enough to keep permanently as a test oracle.

## 6. Ownership is checkable independently of the hand pattern

The ownership rule can fail even when the claimed hand plainly exists, and
it fails most often on the categories that consume all five cards with no
free slot: Royal Flush, Straight Flush, and Full House. Those three have no
kicker to swap in a claimant card, so ownership must hold on the core
itself. Dry run D below is exactly this case.

# Part 2 — Dry runs

Card notation `8♠`; owner in parentheses. Canonical card order is rank
ascending, then suit `♣ < ♦ < ♥ < ♠`.

## Dry run A — 5 players, round 1, truthful claim

Seats A..E, all `H=1`. Pool = 5 cards, so exactly one 5-subset exists.

```
A: 8♠   B: 8♥   C: K♦   D: 3♣   E: A♦
Classify(pool) = Pair, rank 8  (ranks 8,8,K,3,A -> 2+1+1+1)

Starter A claims "Pair of 8s"        (ordinal 14, legal: no standing claim)
B's turn: raise above 14, or SHOW.
B calls SHOW.

Validation: the single 5-subset contains A's 8♠  -> ownership OK
            Classify = Pair rank 8 == claim       -> TRUE
Result: claim TRUE -> the SHOW caller loses.
        B: H 1 -> 2.
Round 2 starter = B  (rotation is independent of the SHOW result).
```

Note B lost while holding an 8 and knowing the pair existed. Calling SHOW
into a pool of 5 is close to a coin flip, which is the intended tension.

## Dry run B — exclusion of an extra matching card

Round 2. `H`: A=1, B=2, C=1, D=1, E=1 → pool = 6.

```
A: 8♠   B: 8♥ 8♦   C: K♣   D: 3♣   E: A♦

Starter B claims "Pair of 8s".
Subsets are the 6 five-card selections (each excludes one card).
  exclude 8♠ -> {8♥,8♦,K♣,3♣,A♦} -> ranks 8,8,K,3,A -> Pair rank 8  ✓
  contains B's 8♥ and 8♦                                            ✓
Result: TRUE.
```

Contrast with a 5-card pool `{8♠,8♥,8♦,K♣,3♣}`: the only subset classifies
as **Three of a Kind**, so `Pair of 8s` is **FALSE**. Same three 8s, opposite
verdict. This pair of cases is the canonical regression test for
`show-validation.md` §2 and is the case your §12 illustration gets wrong.

## Dry run C — your §26 Two Pair scenario

6 players, all `H=1`.

```
A: 2♠   B: 2♥   C: A♣   D: 4♦   E: 4♠   F: 4♣
Claimant A (owns 2♠) claims "Two Pair — 4s and 2s"
  (canonical: high=4, low=2; your wording "2s and 4s" normalises to this)

Deterministic witness = lexicographically smallest satisfying subset
  = {2♥, 2♠, 4♣, 4♦, A♣}      (excludes 4♠)
  ranks 2,2,4,4,A -> 2+2+1 -> Two Pair, high 4, low 2   ✓
  contains A's 2♠                                        ✓
Result: TRUE.
```

Had the selection kept all three 4s it would classify as a Full House and
fail the match — the exclusion is mandatory, not optional.

## Dry run D — ownership failure with the hand plainly present

6 players, all `H=1`.

```
A: 2♣   B: 7♠   C: 7♥   D: 7♦   E: 8♠   F: 8♥

A claims "Full House — 7s full of 8s"
  Core is forced: {7♠,7♥,7♦,8♠,8♥} — exactly 5 cards, all owned by others.
  No alternative selection exists (only three 7s, only two 8s).
  Ownership fails -> FALSE. A loses the SHOW.

A claims "Three 7s" instead
  Core {7♠,7♥,7♦} + 2 fillers of distinct ranks != 7.
  Filler candidates: 2♣(A), 8♠, 8♥.
  {8♠,8♥} is rejected — equal ranks would make it a Full House.
  {2♣,8♠} works -> S = {2♣,7♦,7♥,7♠,8♠} -> Trips 7s, contains A's 2♣ -> TRUE.
```

One claim FALSE and one TRUE over an identical pool, separated only by the
ownership rule and filler distinctness. Both directions are tested.

## Dry run E — declared flush high card must be the maximum of the selection

```
Pool hearts: A♥ Q♥ J♥ 7♥ 3♥   (plus irrelevant off-suit cards)

Claim "Flush of Hearts — Queen high"
  Candidates = hearts of rank <= Q = {Q♥,J♥,7♥,3♥} = 4 cards < 5
  -> FALSE, even though a 5-card heart flush exists (it is Ace-high).

Add 5♥ to the pool:
  {Q♥,J♥,7♥,5♥,3♥} -> max = Q ✓, not 5 consecutive ✓ -> Flush, Q high
  -> TRUE (given claimant ownership of one of those five).
```

This is your §16 requirement, and it needs no special-case code: `Classify`
derives the high card from the selection itself.

## Dry run F — straight shadowed by straight flush

```
Pool = {5♠,6♠,7♠,8♠,9♠}   (exactly 5 cards)
  Claim "Straight to 9"                  -> FALSE (classifies as Straight Flush)
  Claim "Straight Flush to 9 of Spades"  -> TRUE

Pool = {5♠,6♠,7♠,8♠,9♠,9♥}   (6 cards)
  Selection {5♠,6♠,7♠,8♠,9♥} -> mixed suits -> Straight, high 9
  Claim "Straight to 9"                  -> TRUE
```

Same principle as Dry run B, applied to straights. Also:
`Straight to 5` requires an Ace in the pool (`A,2,3,4,5`), and
`Straight to A` (`10,J,Q,K,A`) fails if the only selection is single-suited,
because that classifies as a Royal Flush.

## Dry run G — the sub-5 pool traces

```
4 players, round 1. H = 1,1,1,1 -> pool = 4.
A claims "Pair of Aces". B calls SHOW.
  No 5-card subset exists -> claim FALSE -> A loses. A: H 1 -> 2.
Round 2: pool = 5. Normal play resumes.
```

The two-player endgame trace is the table in Part 1 §1(b).

## Dry run H — the maximum claim forces SHOW

```
Standing claim = "Royal Flush of Spades" (ordinal 362, the maximum).
Next player's legal-claim range = (362, 362] = empty.
Only legal action: CALL_SHOW.
MAKE_CLAIM -> ERR_CLAIM_NOT_HIGHER.
The engine must surface SHOW as the sole action rather than deadlock.
```

A round is bounded at 363 claims because the claim sequence is strictly
increasing over a 363-element space, so no round can run forever.

## Dry run I — penalty ladder to elimination

```
Losses:  0    1    2    3    4    5
H:       1 -> 2 -> 3 -> 4 -> 5 -> would be 6 -> ELIMINATED
```

Six SHOW losses eliminate a player. No other player's `H` changes at any
step. All cards return to the deck and are reshuffled between every round,
so the penalised player receives entirely new cards, one more than before.

## Dry run J — starter rotation under elimination

Verified against your §47 expected output; the trace table is in
`rules.md` §6. Rotation depends only on seats, eliminations and the round
number — never on who called SHOW, who lied, or who was penalised.
